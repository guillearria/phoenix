# Daylight saving time schedule

**You get:** a day-by-day plan that moves your own wake-up, meal, nap and bedtimes a few minutes at a time around a clock change.

**The problem:** Twice a year parents ask how to move a child's day for the clock change. One asks,
"should we start changing something now?" ([Mumsnet, 2021](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/sleep/4365099-When-clocks-go-back)).
Another: "Gradual change or just straight away?" ([Mumsnet, 2017](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/sleep/3059699-Clocks-go-back)).
Replies suggest 10 or 15 minutes a night but leave the math to the parent. The forum posts are old. The question
comes back every spring and fall, and baby sites still publish new guides for it each year.

**What already exists:** Guides from baby brands ([Hatch](https://www.hatch.co/blog/fall-back-sleep-schedule-chart-2022),
[Taking Cara Babies](https://www.takingcarababies.com/blogs/travel-and-holidays/daylight-savings-babys-sleep))
show one sample chart, usually for a 7 pm bedtime. Hatch also has a time change helper inside its app,
for owners of its sleep gadget. **Why an asker is better off here:** it uses every time in your own day,
lets you pick the pace and before or after, needs no app or gadget, and prints.

**What is missing:**
- One routine at a time. Two children on different schedules need two tabs.
- No "half before, half after" option, and no calendar file.
- It does arithmetic only. It gives no sleep or health advice.

**Pass mark:** 10 people say they used it for a real clock change by 2026-11-22. Counted from issues,
comments or messages that say so.

**Status:** live
